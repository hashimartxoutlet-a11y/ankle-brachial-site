Our Educational Mission

Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) affects over 230 million individuals globally, yet it remains one of the most chronically underdiagnosed manifestations of systemic atherosclerotic vascular disease. A major barrier to timely diagnosis is confusion surrounding non-invasive bedside hemodynamic measurements, specifically the Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI) and its complementary modality, the Toe-Brachial Index (TBI).

Founded and curated by Hashir Ali, the Ankle-Brachial platform was designed from the ground up to solve three fundamental challenges in digital health communication:

1

Algorithmic Precision

Strict mathematical adherence to the official AHA/ACC formula: selecting the higher of the dorsalis pedis or posterior tibial pressures over the highest systemic brachial pressure.

2

Privacy By Architecture

100% client-side script execution. Zero database storage of blood pressure readings, ensuring patient confidentiality is protected by design.

3

Accessible Global Guidance

Comprehensive clinical explanations and educational guidance based on international cardiovascular consensus guidelines (AHA, ACC, NICE).

Clinical Review & Evidence-Based Standards

All educational statements, reference tables, and interactive diagnostic interpretations on this platform are cross-referenced with foundational consensus literature:

  • 2016 AHA/ACC Guideline on the Management of Patients With Lower Extremity Peripheral Artery Disease: Standardizing the 1.00–1.40 normal range and the ≤ 0.90 diagnostic threshold.
  • AHA Scientific Statement on Measurement and Interpretation of the Ankle-Brachial Index (Aboyans et al.): Establishing continuous-wave Doppler ultrasound protocols and multi-cuff inflation procedures.
  • Society for Vascular Surgery (SVS) Practice Guidelines: Outlining the clinical necessity of Toe-Brachial Index (TBI) testing in patients with diabetes and end-stage renal disease (ESRD).
  • Stanford Medicine 25 Bedside Physical Exam Initiatives: Refining patient positioning (supine rest) and acoustic signal identification (triphasic vs monophasic).
