Standard ABI Educational Reference Classification

ABI Range Classification General Interpretation Recommended Medical Action
1.00 – 1.40 Normal range Adequate lower-extremity arterial blood perfusion. Routine preventive cardiovascular health maintenance.
0.91 – 0.99 Borderline Equivocal perfusion; may indicate early or mild flow impedance. Discuss with doctor; consider post-exercise treadmill testing.
0.41 – 0.90 Possible reduced blood flow Associated with mild to moderate arterial obstruction or claudication. Schedule a medical consultation for vascular evaluation.
0.00 – 0.40 Severely reduced range May indicate critical limb ischemia or severe arterial blockage. Prompt medical evaluation and urgent vascular specialist review.
> 1.40 Non-compressible arteries Stiff or calcified vessel walls prevent normal cuff occlusion. Physician evaluation; alternative tests like Toe-Brachial Index (TBI).

* Based on American Heart Association (AHA) and Society for Vascular Surgery (SVS) clinical practice guidelines for the assessment of peripheral arterial disease.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, evidence-grounded answers to common questions about ankle-brachial index testing.

What is an ankle-brachial index?

The ankle-brachial index (ABI) is an objective non-invasive vascular test comparing the systolic blood pressure measured at the ankle with the systolic blood pressure measured in the upper arm (brachial artery). It provides clinical insight into arterial circulation reaching the lower extremities.

How is ABI calculated?

ABI is calculated by dividing the systolic blood pressure at the ankle by the highest systolic blood pressure measured between both arms. The formula is: Leg ABI = Ankle Systolic Pressure ÷ Highest Arm Systolic Pressure.

Why is the higher arm pressure used?

Using the higher arm systolic reading provides the most reliable estimate of central systemic arterial perfusion pressure. If one subclavian or brachial artery has mild stenosis, the lower arm reading would underestimate systemic pressure and falsely inflate the calculated ankle ratio.

What is considered a normal ABI range?

An ABI between 1.00 and 1.40 is widely considered the normal reference range, suggesting adequate arterial blood flow to the lower extremity under resting conditions.

Can this calculator diagnose peripheral artery disease?

No. This online calculator is strictly an educational tool. A medical diagnosis of peripheral artery disease (PAD) requires an in-person clinical assessment by a licensed healthcare provider, standard continuous-wave Doppler probe examination, waveform analysis, and comprehensive cardiovascular history.

What does an ABI above 1.40 mean?

An ABI greater than 1.40 generally indicates non-compressible, calcified, or stiffened arterial walls (often referred to as medial arterial calcification or Mönckeberg's arteriosclerosis). It is commonly seen in patients with long-standing diabetes or chronic kidney disease and usually warrants alternative diagnostic evaluations, such as a toe-brachial index (TBI).

When should I talk to a healthcare professional?

You should speak with a qualified physician or vascular specialist if you experience leg pain while walking (claudication), unexplained numbness, persistent coldness in one foot, non-healing sores or ulcers on your feet or toes, or if your readings consistently fall outside the normal 1.00 to 1.40 range.

Is this calculator storing my blood pressure readings?

No. This tool does not store, transmit, log, or share any blood pressure numbers or calculated results. All mathematical evaluations run 100% locally in your device's web browser, maintaining total privacy.
